Business analysis case study

Safarny: making visa requirements clear for Egyptian travelers

A web and mobile platform that tells travelers which visa they need, which documents to prepare and where to get them in Egypt, then checks their documents and books the embassy appointment.

01 · Problem & goals

The traveler carries all the risk of getting the paperwork wrong

The United States alone has around 20 visa types. Egyptian travelers piece together rules from embassy sites, forums, agencies and social media. Wrong submissions lead to rejections, and agency staff spend their time explaining the same rules again and again.

03 · Discovery

A survey to size the problem, interviews to understand it

31 people answered the questionnaire. Three structured interviews covered a frequent business traveler, a student who had been refused a US visa, and a travel agency. I turned each interview into a persona.

What travelers told usn = 31
  • Want visa types and required documents in one place96.8%
  • Would trust an app to book their embassy appointment87.1%
  • Rate visa requirements 3 out of 5 or lower for clarity74.2%
  • Always or often research visa requirements online71.0%
  • Plan to apply for a tourism visa (29% for a student visa)58.1%

Respondents rated the US visa hardest to research, followed by Schengen and Germany.

05 · Scope

What crosses the system boundary

The context diagram fixed scope early. Safarny doesn't sell flights or submit visas itself: it guides, checks and books, and hands travel sales to partner agencies.

In scope · release 1
  • Web app and mobile app for travelers in Egypt
  • Visa guidance per destination and visa type
  • Document checklist and where to obtain each item
  • Document upload and admin review
  • Embassy appointment booking and reminders
  • Agency bundles and 6-month / yearly subscriptions
Out of scope · later
  • Selling flights or hotels directly
  • Submitting visa applications for the traveler
  • Travel insurance
  • Document translation
  • Markets outside Egypt and extra languages

Acceptance criteria · US-02

Document checklist

Given I selected "Germany" and "Tourism visa"
When  I open the requirements page
Then  every required document appears as a checklist item
And   each item shows where it is issued in Egypt
And   the items I tick are saved to my account
Acceptance criteria · US-06

Appointment booking

Given an admin approved my uploaded documents
When  I choose an open embassy slot
Then  the slot is reserved and I get a confirmation email
And   a reminder is sent 48 hours before the appointment

Given a required document is missing
When  I try to book
Then  booking is blocked and I see which document to fix

Cards are sub-tasks; the tag shows the parent story. As the BA, I write the acceptance criteria before a story enters a sprint, and I check each story against them in the "In review" column before it moves to Done.

Definition of Ready
  • Written as a user story with a clear "so that"
  • Acceptance criteria in Given / When / Then
  • Linked to its epic, use case and evidence
  • Estimated in story points by the team
  • No open questions for stakeholders
Definition of Done
  • All acceptance criteria pass in review
  • Meets the related NFRs (speed, security, accessibility)
  • Data changes match the ERD
  • Demoed at the sprint review
  • Jira issue closed and traceability updated
11 · Outcome

What I delivered, what I learned, what comes next

Evidence beats opinion

Tying every story to a survey figure or interview made scope discussions shorter and prioritization easy to defend.

Models expose gaps

Drawing the process and data flows surfaced the admin document review before any code existed. It became a core feature.

Say what's out

A written out-of-scope list and a "Won't" priority kept a 6-person student team focused on the flow that mattered.
